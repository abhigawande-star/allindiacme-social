## A. Fixed brand rules — founder only

No worker edits this section. The Standards Researcher may propose a change in section C.
*Restored 22 Sep 2026 word for word from the founder-set rules recorded in
`claude/social-media-strategy.md` §4 and the safety rules in `claude/social-content-machine.md`.*

1. **Clinical accuracy outranks engagement.** A dose, caveat or contraindication is never cut to
   make a line read better. Never sacrifice medical credibility for reach.
2. **CME first; masterclass is the format, not the product** (founder, 22 Sep 2026). What All
   India CME offers is CME. The masterclass is how that CME is taught: longitudinal, one skill,
   twenty-four sessions a year. Say "CME in masterclass format", "a year-long CME programme",
   "CME, taught the masterclass way", "a CME masterclass". Never present "masterclass" as the
   product, never set masterclasses against CME ("masterclasses and cross-specialty CME"), and
   never describe the masterclass without saying it is CME.
3. Sentence case everywhere. **No emoji, ever.**
4. Exactly three hashtags, always `#CME #MedEd` plus one theme tag, after a blank line at the end
   of the caption. No hashtags inside the body.
5. No claim without a number behind it, and no number without a source. No invented statistics.
6. Every image carries the lockup, the tagline *Uniform Excellence in Clinical Practice* and
   *A venture of Covelis Health Tech LLP.* (render.py does this).
7. Grid rhythm navy → ivory → gold across consecutive slots; gold never next to gold.
8. **Not until the founder confirms them:** no CME dates, faculty names, prices, accreditation or
   credit claims, partner names, and no CME promotion posts.
9. Every clinical claim is tied to a named, current guideline that both the Factory and the
   Guardian opened. Indian body first (IAP, FOGSI, RSSDI, ICMR, ISA, ISCCM, NNF), else WHO or the
   leading international guideline. Generic drug names only.
10. Vignettes are invented and generic. No patient-identifiable detail, however anonymised.
11. Respect protocol (Architecture §12): professional, courteous, never shaming, never
    manipulative, no pressure.

## B. Strategy — the Standards Researcher edits, with date and source

- **Cadence:** 2 posts a day, 09:30 and 17:30 IST. Both slots sit on the strongest windows in
  Buffer's 9.6M-post analysis (9 a.m. and 6 p.m. local time); keep them.
- **Day weighting (24 Sep 2026):** Wednesday and Thursday are the strongest days, Friday and
  Saturday the weakest (Buffer, 9.6M posts, Jan 2024 – Dec 2025). Volume does not change; placement
  does. Put the week's strongest clinical post or carousel on Wednesday or Thursday, and put brand
  and lighter educational posts on Friday and Saturday.
- **Mix (share of output):** clinical knowledge 40%, educational 25%, brand 20%, CME promotion 10%
  (paused until A8 clears — use clinical or educational instead), post-CME 5% (none yet).
  AYUSH-relevant posts sit inside these pillars; aim for about one in seven.
- **Rotation:** no topic or headline repeated within the last 60 posts. Rotate specialties:
  obstetrics and gynaecology, anaesthesia and pain, paediatrics and neonatology, diabetes and
  internal medicine, cardiology, orthopaedics, radiology, AYUSH.
- **Clinical post shape:** one actionable point, the caveat that matters, source on the image or
  in the caption.
- **The shape that earns shares (24 Sep 2026):** in the one published analysis of a
  clinician-facing medical-education account, educational posts — board-review cases, clinical
  infographics, annotated images — drew the most shares, while people-and-milestone posts drew the
  most likes and comments. We optimise for shares, so build the clinical post as a case or a
  question with the answer and the pearl, not as a statement.
- **Question and knowledge shape (3 Oct 2026; extended 5 Oct 2026):** when a post asks something,
  ask it as a multiple-choice question. In the one controlled comparison available, MCQs drew
  significantly more participation than yes/no or open-ended prompts (p < 0.001), and within
  knowledge posts mnemonics and practical general information substantially outperformed
  clinical-trial and case-report write-ups. The mnemonic half of that is now replicated in a
  randomised comparison: mnemonic posts drew significantly more engagement than clinical-trial
  posts (p = 0.01), and adding supplementary visuals — comics, extra images — to a trial write-up
  produced no improvement at all (p = 0.325). So lead with the retainable pearl, and do not dress a
  study summary up to compensate for one. **Scope limit:** both cohorts were medical students, not
  practising clinicians, so treat this as the default shape for our MCQ carousels and not as
  evidence about consultant audiences. Sources in section C.
- **Formats from 9 Oct 2026** (revised 24 Sep 2026; old rule and evidence in section C): per week
  6 single squares, 4 carousels (2 reference, ≤10 slides, and 2 MCQ question), and 2 Reels once a
  Reels route exists. **Reels run ≤60 seconds** (added 3 Oct 2026). Never two carousels back to
  back; not on CME day or the day before.
  Carousels need the Publisher's carousel flow and Reels need a video route. Until each is tested,
  draft single squares only **and record the unmet slot** — a missing carousel or Reel is a
  capability gap to escalate, not a slot to fill silently.
  **Slide count is not the lever (5 Oct 2026):** in the one randomised comparison, single-slide and
  multi-slide posts drew equivalent engagement. The carousel allocation above rests on saves, not
  on length, so a carousel has to be worth saving rather than merely longer — and a two-slide
  carousel that earns a save beats a ten-slide one that does not.
  **Format advantage is a scale effect (12 Oct 2026):** in an independent 35M-post dataset
  (447,613 pages, Jan–Dec 2025), carousels earn the most saves at every account size, but the gap
  only opens with size — at 1–5K followers carousels, Reels and single images all average one save
  per post; at 10–50K it is 8 saves against 3 for images, at 50–100K 35 against 10. Single-image
  engagement rate fell from 0.45% to 0.37% across 2024–2025 and sits at 0.33% in Q2 2026, which
  corroborates Metricool's single-image reach decline from a second dataset. The weekly allocation
  is deliberately *unchanged*: this bounds it rather than overturning it. At our size no format
  earns saves, so the split stays preparation — the same finding as the reality check, reached from
  a different direction. Source in section C.
- **Optimise for** shares and saves, not likes. The confirmed ranking signals are watch time,
  likes per reach and sends per reach; a send is weighted several times a like and a save about
  three times a like.
  **The two levers split by format (15 Oct 2026):** in a second Socialinsider dataset — 15,000,000
  posts from 417,130 pages, Oct 2025 – Mar 2026 — carousels lead on saves (median 37, save rate
  0.05%) while Reels lead on shares (median 5 shares, share rate 0.10%, against carousels' 3 and
  0.08%); single images trail on both (median 10 saves and 1 share, 0.02% and 0.07%). So the
  carousel slot is the saves lever and the Reels slot is the shares lever — and because a send is
  weighted several times a like, the Reels route we have not built is the gap on our *stronger*
  signal, not the lesser one. Record the unmet Reel slot on that footing. These are medians across
  all industries with no healthcare breakdown and no account-size split for saves or shares, so
  this ranks the formats without sizing the effect at our scale; the weekly allocation is
  unchanged. Source in section C.
- **Compliance constraints (24 Sep 2026; NMC social-media guidance for registered medical
  practitioners, 13 Aug 2023):** no patient photographs or scan images; no before-and-after,
  healed-patient, surgery or procedure imagery, however impressive the result; nothing that
  solicits patients — every post is clinician-facing; never buy likes, followers or ranking.
  These sit alongside A10 and A11 and bind the Factory and the Guardian equally.
- **Compliance constraints — second instrument (8 Oct 2026; NMC Ethics and Medical Registration
  Board, *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication by Hospitals/Medical
  Institutions and Registered Medical Practitioners*, public notice dated 6 Oct 2026, in force
  immediately).** This sits alongside the 24 Sep bullet above and replaces nothing in it.
  - **Advertising is defined broadly.** Any communication that directly or indirectly promotes the
    professional services, reputation or qualifications of a practitioner or an institution counts
    as advertising, and a communication is treated as advertising whenever its content *or its
    manner of dissemination* has promotional character. Factual information remains permitted.
  - **The exemption we sit inside, and must keep sitting inside.** Health-awareness programmes,
    public-health campaigns, academic discussion and educational content are expressly permitted
    provided they do not promote personal practice, do not solicit patients, and are not monetised
    through promotional marketing. So every post stays clinician-facing education that solicits no
    patient. That is now a compliance position, not only a style choice.
    **The provision named (19 Oct 2026).** In the notice's own text the exemption rests on
    Explanation I to para 3.2, which places "the discourse of information in the normal course of
    discharge of information to the public at large" outside the *definition* of advertisement
    ("discourse" is an extraction artefact for "disclosure"). Non-promotional information with no
    commercial intent is therefore outside the definition rather than an allowance inside it — a
    stronger position than a carve-out, and one that holds only while the content stays
    non-promotional in substance and in manner. One secondary account reads the Explanations as
    covering government campaigns only; the primary text does not. Source in section C.
  - **No superlatives, no guarantees.** Never "best", "No. 1" or "leading" unless objectively
    verifiable; never a guaranteed cure, a success rate or a patient-number claim. Any ranking or
    award quoted must be independently verifiable, with its methodology and date.
  - **No fear-based framing,** and nothing written to create unnecessary demand for a procedure or
    an investigation. This binds the clinical pillar: a post may state a risk that a named current
    guideline states, and may never manufacture alarm around it.
  - **Engagement integrity, widened.** The 24 Sep rule was "never buy likes, followers or ranking".
    It now reads: never procure or manipulate fake followers, likes, reviews or ratings, and never
    manipulate search rankings or algorithms to create a misleading impression of standing.
  - **AI.** AI-generated promotional campaigns furthering a commercial interest are prohibited, and
    permitted AI-generated promotional content must carry a clear disclosure that its origin is AI.
    Our cards are machine-rendered and our captions machine-drafted, so our protection is the
    exemption above: keep every post educational and non-promotional in content and in framing.
    Never use AI to create or alter a patient, practitioner, image, voice, testimonial or clinical
    outcome. Whether the brand pillar clears this line is a founder call — see section C.
    **Second disclosure limb (15 Oct 2026).** A fifth independent source, a specialist technology-
    policy outlet, describes a duty that sits beside the promotional one: where AI-generated or
    materially AI-altered content could mislead the audience about its artificial nature, the
    content must reasonably disclose that it is AI-generated or AI-assisted. That limb is not on
    its face confined to promotional content. Our cards are rendered type and brand furniture, not
    synthetic people, voices, scans or clinical images, so the misleading-about-artificial-nature
    trigger is doubtful on our output as it stands — but it is doubtful, not excluded. The
    educational exemption stays the primary protection, the 8 Oct founder proposal about an
    AI-origin line beside the lockup is strengthened rather than resolved, and this binds hard the
    moment any post carries a generated image of a person, a voice or a clinical picture. Source
    in section C.
    **Primary text reached, and a third limb (19 Oct 2026).** The clause five secondary sources
    paraphrased can now be read. A copy of the notice itself — *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising
    and Public Communication by hospitals/medical institutions and Registered Medical
    Practitioners*, NMC Ethics & Medical Registration Board, Ref. No. R-13014/01/2024-Ethics,
    dated 06.10.2026, in force from 6 Oct 2026 — is posted on ResearchGate, and para 7.2 of
    Chapter II runs to lettered sub-clauses rather than the two sentences the roundups quoted.
    Two of them reach us. **7.2(h)** reads, in that copy, that "[w]here Al-generated or materially
    Al-altered content is used" and "its artificial nature may reasonably be material to the
    audience's understanding", "appropriate disclosure shall be made" — so the 15 Oct second limb
    is confirmed as a separately lettered duty and is not on its face promotional-only. **7.2(i)**
    is new to us: "[a]ny use of patient information as input for an Al system" "shall comply with
    applicable privacy, confidentiality, data-protection" "and professional-ethics requirements."
    Operationally: never place patient information of any kind into a drafting, rendering or
    research prompt. Our vignettes are invented and generic already (A10), so this records a rule
    we keep rather than imposing a new one. The copy is a third-party upload carrying no NMC
    letterhead, so it is the primary text in substance and not an official source; a founder-grade
    reading should still be taken from the NMC site. Source in section C.
  - **When A8 clears and faculty are named,** a named practitioner's post must carry their name,
    qualifications, registration status and registration number, and an institution must disclose
    the details of the practitioners associated with its posts. Engaging an agency, an influencer
    or a platform transfers no responsibility for anything published.
  - **Patient imagery does not loosen.** The guidelines allow before-and-after and case imagery for
    scientific or educational use with anonymised consent. Our own rule is stricter and does not
    move: none of it, ever (24 Sep bullet, A10).
- **Reality check (updated 19 Oct 2026):** across all 50 posts old enough to measure, reach is
  0–11 accounts per post with a median of 4, and there are zero saves, zero shares and zero
  comments on every one of them, plus 69 likes and two profile visits in total. The floor has
  fallen through one: the 16 Oct 12:25 open-fracture post reached nobody at all, the first
  zero-reach post in the account's history, and the ten posts that have become measurable since
  the 12 Oct run sit at reach 0–5. Cadence alone is still shown not to build distribution, and six
  weeks of it has moved the line down, not up. Format tuning
  cannot fix a problem of that size. Until reach is consistently in the hundreds, treat these
  format rules as preparation, and name distribution — not format — as the binding constraint in
  each Guardian run. **The route is now evidenced (5 Oct 2026):** in a
  randomised medical-education account study, a passive asynchronous invitation produced 3
  followers while personalised direct invitations produced 30 within twelve hours. Direct personal
  invitation, not publishing volume, is what builds the first audience. Acting on it is a founder
  call (see section C); quoting it is not.
  **An outside number for the scale of the gap (19 Oct 2026):** the one published prospective
  account of a medical-education account's own social output — 45 Instagram posts and 23 X posts,
  May 2022 to July 2025 — averaged 13 likes and 473 impressions per Instagram post, grew from 170
  to 449 followers, and saw website traffic significantly higher while posts were live
  (p = 0.012). Two readings, both ours to use: a clinician-facing education account of ordinary
  size runs two orders of magnitude above our reach of 4, which is where the ceiling sits once an
  audience exists; and that account began with 170 followers, which is the asset we do not have.
  It is a single account, its users were 74% healthcare-professional students, and it reports
  nothing by post format, so it calibrates the gap and settles no format question. Source in
  section C.
